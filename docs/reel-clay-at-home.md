# Reel: "What I learnt about clay today"

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 4 clips of about 8 seconds each (≈32–36 s total), joined with one voiceover and background music.
**Story:** Two school children come home and tell their family what they learnt about clay pots. The family's Mudkraft pots are in the kitchen around them.
**Tone:** warm, curious, proud of tradition. The children talk about what clay *does*. We never say anything bad about steel, aluminium or any other material. When metal is mentioned at all, it is only as "other utensils" in a neutral way, and preferably not at all.

**Cast**
- **Riya**, about 10, school uniform, plaits, carries a notebook.
- **Aarav**, about 7, her younger brother, school uniform.
- **Maa**, in her 30s, cooking.
- **Dadi**, the grandmother, in her 60s.

---

## The rule: every claim on screen has a source

| # | Claim used in the reel | Proof (shown as a small on-screen caption) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indians have made clay pots for over 4,500 years, since Harappan times. | NCERT Class 12 History, *Themes in Indian History I*, Ch. 1 "Bricks, Beads and Bones" (Harappan pottery) |
| 2 | India's national nutrition institute calls earthen pots the safest cookware: eco-friendly, needing less oil, and keeping nutrients. | ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition, *Dietary Guidelines for Indians*, 2024 ([PDF](https://www.nin.res.in/dietaryguidelines/pdfjs/locale/DGI24thJune2024fin.pdf)) |
| 3 | A clay matka cools water without electricity: water seeps through the tiny pores and evaporates, and evaporation cools what is left. | NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch. 1 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (evaporation causes cooling); Joshi et al., IOP Conf. Ser. 2018 |
| 4 | Mudkraft clay was lab-tested: no lead, cadmium or arsenic detected; pH 7.2. | CGCRI report Ref. CGCRI/KHRJ/2026-03-118, mudkraft.com/soil-report |
| 5 | Wood-fired for 48 hours at 920–1040 °C. Unglazed, so food touches only fired clay. | Same CGCRI report and Mudkraft's own process |
| 6 | Clay holds heat and lets steam breathe, so food simmers slowly. | Physical property of porous fired clay (12.8% water absorption, CGCRI report) |

**Do not say** any of these. They are common online but we have no solid proof, or they compare against other materials:
"alkaline food", "neutralises acidity", "adds minerals / iron to food", "boosts immunity", "aluminium is toxic", "metal is harmful".
**Taste** is said only as a *feeling* ("Dadi says it tastes like her childhood"). It is never presented as a fact.

> Before the edit is locked, open the NIN PDF and note the exact page with the earthenware line. Put that page number in caption 2 (e.g. "ICMR–NIN DGI 2024, p. __").

## Assembly notes

1. **Clips:** Generate each clip from its prompt (Veo, Kling, Runway, etc.). Give the tool the listed product images as reference frames so the pots look like *our* pots. Keep the same four characters in all clips: generate Clip 1 first, then use a frame from it as the character reference for Clips 2–4.
2. **Real footage beats AI for the products.** If possible, shoot the close-ups of the pots (steam, moisture beads, lid lift) on a phone, and use AI only for the family scenes.
3. **Order:** Clip 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, with 6–8 frame cross-dissolves, or match-cut on steam.
4. **Audio:** Soft tanpura or flute bed at about −18 dB, VO at about −6 dB, plus light kitchen sounds (simmer, pour, clink of a kulhad).
5. **Captions:** Burn in captions (most reels are watched muted). Source captions go bottom-left in small type (about 24 px at 1080 w), shown for at least 2 s each.
6. **Export:** 1080×1920, H.264, 30 fps, under 60 s.
7. **Instagram caption:** Put all the sources from the table above in the post caption, with a link to mudkraft.com/soil-report.
